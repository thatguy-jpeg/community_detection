How to pick k

  1. Remove edges. After each split, write down Q.
  2. Keep going until every node is alone.
  3. Choose the k with the highest Q.

Betweenness

b(e)=∑s<tσst(e)σst

σst = number of shortest paths from s to t. σst(e) = how many of them use edge e. A bridge between two groups sits on almost every cross-group path, so its b is large. Tied paths count fractionally.

Modularity

Q=∑c(lcm−(dc2m)2)

m = edges in the original graph. lc = original edges inside community c. dc = sum of original degrees in c. First term: how many edges fall inside c. Second term: how many you would expect by chance (null model). Q is always between −1 and 1.

Exam notes

Girvan–Newman

  • Divisive hierarchical clustering. Remove the edge with the highest betweenness. Repeat. Connected components are communities.
  • Recompute betweenness after every removal. Old values go stale.
  • Choose the number of clusters at the peak of Q.
  • Q uses the original graph's m and degrees, not the shrinking graph.
  • Slow: every step needs shortest paths from every node.

Spectral

  • L = D − A. L is symmetric. Its eigenvalues are ≥ 0. The smallest is 0 (all-ones eigenvector).
  • Use the eigenvector of the second-smallest eigenvalue λ₂. Split at 0 (or at the median).
  • It approximates the minimum normalized cut. Plain min-cut ignores cluster size and can peel off one node.
  • For k clusters: recurse on bi-partitions, or cluster several eigenvectors (preferred).
